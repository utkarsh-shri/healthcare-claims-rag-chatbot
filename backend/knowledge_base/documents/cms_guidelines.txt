CMS MEDICARE PART D COVERAGE RULES AND GUIDELINES
Healthcare Claims Knowledge Base | Version 2024.1 | Source: CMS.gov Part D Policy

This document summarizes Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) guidelines governing Medicare Part D prescription drug benefit coverage, formulary requirements, low-income subsidy (LIS) provisions, and the transition fill policy. Intended for use by PBM operations teams, claims processors, and pharmacy benefit administrators.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PART I: MEDICARE PART D OVERVIEW

Medicare Part D is the voluntary outpatient prescription drug benefit program established under the Medicare Prescription Drug, Improvement, and Modernization Act of 2003 (MMA). Part D benefits are delivered through private Prescription Drug Plans (PDPs) or Medicare Advantage Prescription Drug Plans (MA-PDs) that contract with CMS.

Key Program Parameters (2024):
  - Annual Deductible Maximum: $545 (standard benefit)
  - Initial Coverage Limit (ICL): $5,030 in total drug costs
  - Out-of-Pocket Threshold (OOPC): $8,000 (eliminates catastrophic coinsurance in 2024)
  - Catastrophic Coverage: After OOPC, member pays $0 for covered Part D drugs (2024 Inflation Reduction Act change)
  - Coverage Gap ("Donut Hole"): Effectively eliminated in 2024 for most standard plans

Plan Sponsors must offer either the defined standard benefit or an actuarially equivalent alternative. Enhanced benefit plans may offer additional coverage beyond the standard.

PART II: FORMULARY REQUIREMENTS

CMS requires all Part D plan sponsors to maintain a formulary that meets the following requirements:

2.1 Minimum Drug Coverage — Six Protected Classes
Part D plans MUST cover all or substantially all drugs in these six therapeutic categories:
  1. Immunosuppressants (for prevention of organ transplant rejection)
  2. Antidepressants
  3. Antipsychotics
  4. Anticonvulsants (seizure medications)
  5. Antiretrovirals (HIV/AIDS medications)
  6. Antineoplastics (cancer medications)
No plan may impose restrictions on these six classes that are not based on clinical grounds and approved by CMS.

2.2 Formulary Review and Approval
  - Plans must submit formularies to CMS annually for review.
  - Mid-year formulary changes that restrict access (tier moves, PA additions, QL additions) require CMS approval and 60-day member advance notice.
  - Adding a drug to the formulary or removing restrictions does not require advance notice.
  - Formularies are published on Medicare Plan Finder (medicare.gov).

2.3 Non-Formulary Drug Access
If a drug is not on the formulary, members may:
  (a) Request a formulary exception for medical necessity (see Part VI).
  (b) Pay cash price out-of-pocket (does not count toward TrOOP).
  (c) Receive a transition fill (see Part IV).

2.4 Cost-Sharing Requirements
  - Plans must place generics in Tier 1 or Tier 2 (preferred generics in Tier 1).
  - Plans may not place a drug in a higher cost-sharing tier solely to discourage use.
  - Specialty drugs (typically >$670/month threshold in 2024) may be placed on a Specialty Tier with up to 33% coinsurance.
  - Insulin products: Cost sharing capped at $35/month per covered insulin product (IRA 2022).

PART III: PRIOR AUTHORIZATION IN PART D

3.1 Authority to Require PA
Part D plans may require prior authorization as a utilization management tool when:
  - The drug has a high risk of abuse or misuse.
  - The drug is indicated only for specific diagnoses.
  - Clinical guidelines recommend step therapy before use.
  - The drug has a narrow therapeutic index.

3.2 PA Timeframes (Regulatory Requirements)
  - Standard PA decision: Within 72 hours of receipt of request.
  - Expedited (urgent) PA decision: Within 24 hours when standard timeline would seriously jeopardize health.
  - If the plan fails to meet these timeframes, the request is deemed approved.

3.3 PA Denial Rights
  When a PA is denied, the plan must:
  1. Notify the prescriber and member in writing of the denial.
  2. Provide the specific clinical reason for denial.
  3. Inform the member of the right to appeal.
  4. Include information about the Independent Review Entity (IRE) process.

3.4 Transition Fills During PA Review
  For transition situations (see Part IV), a 30-day emergency supply must be provided even if PA has not yet been approved, for non-long-term care members.

PART IV: TRANSITION POLICY

CMS requires Part D plan sponsors to have a transition policy to assist members who are stabilized on medications that may not be on the new plan's formulary.

4.1 Transition Eligibility
A transition fill is required for:
  - New Part D enrollees who are currently taking drugs not on the plan's formulary.
  - Members who experience a mid-year formulary change that removes their drug.
  - Members transitioning from other coverage (e.g., Medicaid to Part D).

4.2 Transition Supply Requirements
  - Retail pharmacy: At least one 30-day supply per drug during the transition period.
  - Long-term care (LTC) pharmacy: At least a 31-day supply, or a lesser supply if the dispensing unit is smaller (e.g., 7-day supply for LTC with daily pill dispense).
  - Transition period: 90 days from enrollment effective date.

4.3 Transition Notice
  - The plan must send a written notice to the member within 3 business days of the transition fill.
  - Notice must explain that the drug is not covered, the right to request a formulary exception, and available alternatives.

4.4 Non-Applicable Situations
  Transition fills are NOT required for:
  - Drugs that are excluded from Part D coverage by statute (see Part V).
  - Drugs on a plan's formulary that simply require PA (standard PA process applies).
  - Members who knowingly enrolled in a plan without checking formulary coverage.

PART V: CMS PART D COVERAGE EXCLUSIONS

By statute and CMS regulation, the following drug categories are excluded from Part D coverage and cannot be covered by the Part D benefit (though they may be covered under Part B or state Medicaid):

5.1 Statutory Exclusions
  1. Drugs covered under Medicare Part A or Part B (e.g., most infusion drugs administered in a clinical setting).
  2. Nonprescription drugs (OTC medications), except as specifically approved by CMS.
  3. Drugs primarily for the treatment of anorexia, weight loss, or weight gain (unless used for a covered indication).
  4. Drugs for promoting fertility.
  5. Drugs for cosmetic purposes or hair growth.
  6. Drugs for the treatment of erectile dysfunction.
  7. Drugs for cough and cold symptoms.
  8. Prescription vitamins and minerals (except prenatal vitamins and fluoride preparations).
  9. Barbiturates (unless used to treat epilepsy, cancer, or chronic mental disorder).
  10. Benzodiazepines (original exclusion; CMS lifted this exclusion effective January 2013 — benzodiazepines ARE now covered by Part D).

Note on Benzodiazepines: Effective January 1, 2013, CMS removed benzodiazepines from the list of excluded Part D drugs. All Part D plans must now cover benzodiazepines. Claims processors must ensure this exclusion code is NOT applied to benzodiazepine claims.

5.2 CMS-Approved Exceptions
Certain drugs may obtain a waiver to be covered on a Part D formulary even if they fall into an exclusion category, provided CMS grants approval during the formulary review process.

PART VI: LOW-INCOME SUBSIDY (LIS / EXTRA HELP)

The Low-Income Subsidy (LIS) program, also known as "Extra Help," provides financial assistance for Part D premiums, deductibles, and cost-sharing for eligible low-income Medicare beneficiaries.

6.1 LIS Eligibility
Full LIS: Income ≤ 135% FPL and resources ≤ $16,660 (single) / $33,240 (couple) — 2024 thresholds.
Partial LIS: Income between 135–150% FPL — sliding scale cost-sharing assistance.
Automatic Eligibility: Members receiving full Medicaid, SSI, or MSP (Medicare Savings Programs) are automatically enrolled.

6.2 LIS Cost-Sharing (2024 Benchmark Plans)
  Full LIS Beneficiaries:
    - Deductible: $0
    - Generic copay: $4.50
    - Brand/non-preferred copay: $11.20
    - Catastrophic phase copay: $0 (2024)

  Partial LIS Beneficiaries:
    - Deductible: $103 (15% of standard deductible)
    - Cost-sharing: 15–25% of drug costs depending on income level

6.3 LIS Formulary Protections
  - LIS members are protected from mid-year formulary changes.
  - If a drug is removed mid-year for an LIS member, the plan must continue coverage through the end of the plan year.
  - LIS members cannot be charged more than the LIS copay maximums regardless of which tier the drug is on.

6.4 Claims Adjudication for LIS Members
  - LIS indicator transmitted in NCPDP field: Other Coverage Code or additional information field.
  - PBM systems must identify LIS status and apply the correct reduced cost-sharing at point of sale.
  - Incorrect application of LIS status results in overcharging the member — a CMS compliance violation.

6.5 Auto-Enrollment and Reassignment
  - CMS auto-enrolls newly eligible full-LIS members into benchmark PDPs each year.
  - LIS members may be reassigned annually if their current plan no longer qualifies as a benchmark.
  - Members must be notified of reassignment by November of the preceding year.

PART VII: MEDICATION THERAPY MANAGEMENT (MTM)

CMS requires Part D sponsors to offer MTM programs to high-risk beneficiaries.

7.1 MTM Eligibility Criteria
Members must meet ALL three criteria:
  1. Have multiple chronic conditions (minimum of 2–3, as defined by the plan).
  2. Take multiple Part D drugs (minimum of 8 recommended by CMS).
  3. Be likely to incur annual drug costs at or above $5,330 (2024 threshold).

7.2 MTM Services Required
  - Comprehensive Medication Review (CMR): Annual, interactive, patient-centered review.
  - Targeted Medication Review (TMR): At least quarterly review with follow-up interventions.
  - Written summaries in plain language provided to the member after each CMR.

PART VIII: CMS REPORTING AND COMPLIANCE

8.1 Prescription Drug Event (PDE) Data
  - All Part D claims must be submitted to CMS as PDE records.
  - PDEs must be submitted within the CMS reporting window (typically 3 months after the service date for interim reconciliation).
  - Inaccurate PDE data is a compliance violation subject to CMS audit and financial penalties.

8.2 Risk Corridors and Reconciliation
  - CMS reconciles premium stabilization payments through risk corridors annually.
  - Sponsors must submit final PDE data for reconciliation by September 30 of the following year.

8.3 Audit and Oversight
  - CMS conducts Program Audits (CPE, IRE, Part D Coverage Determinations) on plan sponsors annually.
  - Plans failing CMS audits may face civil monetary penalties (CMPs) up to $25,000 per member affected.
  - Sponsors must maintain all claims and prior authorization records for a minimum of 10 years.
