FORMULARY TIERS, STEP THERAPY RULES, AND EXCEPTIONS PROCESS
Healthcare Claims Knowledge Base | Version 2024.1

This document defines the formulary tier structure, cost-sharing design, step therapy protocols, quantity limit policies, and the formulary exception and appeal process used in a standard commercial and Medicare Part D pharmacy benefit plan design. Claims processors and pharmacy operations staff should reference this document when responding to formulary-related member and pharmacy inquiries.

PART I: FORMULARY TIER STRUCTURE

A formulary is the plan's list of covered prescription drugs, organized into tiers that determine the member's cost-sharing at the pharmacy.

1.1 Standard Five-Tier Formulary

Tier 1 — Preferred Generic Drugs
  Definition: FDA-approved generic medications that are preferred by the plan for first-line use.
  Cost-Sharing:
    - Commercial plan: Copay typically $5–$15 per 30-day supply.
    - Medicare Part D: Copay typically $4–$10 per 30-day supply (reduced for LIS members).
  Examples: metformin, lisinopril, atorvastatin (generic), omeprazole, metoprolol succinate.
  Key Rule: Plans must preferentially place generics in Tier 1 or Tier 2 per CMS guidelines.

Tier 2 — Non-Preferred Generic / Preferred Brand Drugs
  Definition: Generic drugs not designated as preferred, or brand-name drugs the plan has designated as preferred (typically because of formulary rebate economics).
  Cost-Sharing:
    - Commercial plan: Copay typically $20–$40 per 30-day supply.
    - Medicare Part D: Copay typically $15–$25 per 30-day supply.
  Examples: Preferred brand statins, preferred brand ACE inhibitors, some non-preferred generics.

Tier 3 — Non-Preferred Brand Drugs
  Definition: Brand-name drugs for which a generic or preferred brand alternative exists. Members pay more to incentivize use of lower-cost alternatives.
  Cost-Sharing:
    - Commercial plan: Copay typically $50–$90 per 30-day supply.
    - Medicare Part D: Copay typically $42–$55 per 30-day supply.
  Examples: Brand drugs with AB-rated generic equivalents available (e.g., brand Lipitor when generic atorvastatin is on Tier 1).
  Key Rule: If a member obtains a Tier 3 brand drug via DAW-1 (prescriber requires brand), the member pays the Tier 3 brand copay instead of the generic copay.

Tier 4 — Non-Preferred Brand Drugs (High Cost)
  Definition: Brand-name drugs that are not preferred and do not have preferred alternatives. Often includes drugs the plan discourages due to cost or clinical appropriateness.
  Cost-Sharing:
    - Commercial plan: Copay typically $90–$150 per 30-day supply, OR coinsurance 35–50%.
    - Medicare Part D: Copay typically $90–$100 per 30-day supply.
  Examples: Non-preferred brand drugs without biosimilar, high-cost branded maintenance drugs.

Specialty Tier (Tier 5) — Specialty Drugs
  Definition: High-cost specialty drugs requiring special handling, administration, monitoring, or are primarily dispensed through specialty pharmacies. CMS defines specialty drugs as those with a cost threshold of >$670/month (2024).
  Cost-Sharing:
    - Commercial plan: Coinsurance typically 20–33% per fill (may be subject to an out-of-pocket maximum).
    - Medicare Part D: Coinsurance up to 33% in initial coverage phase (subject to catastrophic phase rules).
  Examples: adalimumab (Humira), lenalidomide (Revlimid), semaglutide (Ozempic), ocrelizumab (Ocrevus), CAR-T therapies.
  Key Rule: CMS limits Medicare Part D specialty coinsurance to ≤33% of the negotiated price.

1.2 Extended Tier Designs
Some commercial plans use additional tiers:
  - Tier 6 (Select Care / Preventive Tier): Certain preventive medications covered at $0 copay per ACA requirements (e.g., statins for primary prevention, PrEP for HIV, contraceptives).
  - Biosimilar Tier: Some plans create a dedicated lower-cost tier for biosimilar drugs to incentivize biosimilar adoption over reference biologics.

PART II: FORMULARY TIER EXCEPTIONS PROCESS

2.1 When a Tier Exception Is Appropriate
A member or prescriber may request a tier exception when:
  - A non-preferred drug is medically necessary because the preferred alternative is clinically inappropriate.
  - The member has tried the preferred drug and experienced an adverse reaction or lack of efficacy.
  - The member has a condition that makes the preferred drug contraindicated.

2.2 Tier Exception Request Requirements
Required documentation:
  1. Completed formulary exception request form (or ePA submission).
  2. Clinical statement from the prescriber documenting:
     - Medical necessity for the non-preferred drug.
     - Specific reason why the preferred alternative cannot be used (contraindication, adverse effect, therapeutic failure).
  3. Supporting clinical documentation (lab results, treatment history, adverse drug reaction documentation).

2.3 Tier Exception Decision Timeframes
  - Standard: 72 hours.
  - Expedited: 24 hours (if member's health is at risk).
  - Approved exception: Member pays the lower (preferred/Tier 1 or Tier 2) cost-sharing for the approved non-preferred drug.
  - Duration: Typically 12 months; must be renewed annually with updated documentation.

2.4 Non-Formulary Exception
  If the drug is not on the formulary at all (not just a higher tier), the member may request a non-formulary exception.
  Requirements are the same as a tier exception but also require:
  - Documentation that no formulary drug in the same therapeutic class is clinically appropriate.
  Approval result: Drug is covered at Tier 3 or Tier 4 cost-sharing (plan-specific).

PART III: STEP THERAPY RULES

Step therapy (also called "step protocol" or "fail-first policy") requires that a member try a less costly or preferred drug before the plan will cover a higher-cost or non-preferred alternative.

3.1 General Step Therapy Principles
  - Step drugs (required first-line drugs) must be available on the formulary and clinically appropriate.
  - Steps are applied at the GPI (Generic Product Identifier) level — not at the specific NDC level.
  - Step therapy is NOT required when the step drug is contraindicated, was previously tried and failed, or the member is currently stable on the non-preferred drug (continuation of therapy).

3.2 Continuity of Care / Grandfathering
  Members currently stabilized on a drug that becomes subject to step therapy mid-year are typically grandfathered (step therapy does not apply) through the end of the plan year. The PBM must capture the prior fill history to apply grandfathering correctly.

3.3 Step Therapy Protocols by Therapeutic Area

Statins (HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors):
  Step 1 (required): Atorvastatin (generic), simvastatin (generic), or pravastatin (generic) — at least 90 days.
  Step 2 (available if Step 1 fails): Rosuvastatin (generic or Crestor).
  Brand statins (e.g., brand Lipitor, brand Crestor): Require Step 1 failure + PA.
  Step Bypass Condition: Statin intolerance (myopathy, rhabdomyolysis), hepatic contraindication, or drug-drug interaction.

Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):
  Step 1: Omeprazole (generic) or pantoprazole (generic) — at least 60 days.
  Step 2: Esomeprazole (generic) or lansoprazole (generic).
  Brand PPIs (e.g., Nexium, Prilosec brand): Require Step 1 and Step 2 failures.
  Step Bypass: Barrett's esophagus, GI bleed, confirmed H. pylori treatment.

TNF Inhibitors / Biologics for Inflammatory Conditions:
  Step 1: Adequate trial of conventional DMARD therapy (see PA criteria document for details).
  Step 2 (Biosimilar preference): Where an FDA-approved biosimilar exists and is preferred, the biosimilar must be tried before the reference biologic.
  Example: Biosimilar adalimumab (Hadlima, Hyrimoz, Cyltezo) before brand Humira.
  Step Bypass: Documented hypersensitivity to biosimilar excipients; clinical instability.

SGLT-2 Inhibitors:
  Step 1: Metformin at maximum tolerated dose (unless eGFR <30 or intolerance).
  Step 2: A sulfonylurea (e.g., glipizide, glimepiride) — for glycemic control indication.
  For cardiovascular/renal indication: SGLT-2 may be covered without step therapy if diagnosis codes reflect HF or CKD.

GLP-1 Receptor Agonists (Diabetes):
  Step 1: Metformin at maximum tolerated dose.
  Step 2: A sulfonylurea OR a DPP-4 inhibitor (e.g., sitagliptin, saxagliptin) — at least 90 days each.
  For high cardiovascular risk patients (ASCVD established): GLP-1 agonists with proven CV benefit (semaglutide, liraglutide) may be approved without step therapy with prescriber documentation.

Opioid Analgesics:
  Step 1: Trial of non-opioid analgesics (NSAIDs, acetaminophen) for non-cancer pain — must be documented.
  For cancer pain or end-of-life care: Step therapy is typically waived.
  Short-acting opioids must be tried before long-acting/extended-release formulations (REMS requirement for ER/LA opioids).

3.4 Step Therapy Override
  If step therapy has been completed and documented but the system does not reflect prior fills (e.g., drugs filled at a different pharmacy or under different coverage), the prescriber must submit attestation of prior therapy failure, and the PBM clinical team can override the step therapy edit manually.

PART IV: QUANTITY LIMITS

4.1 Types of Quantity Limits
  Per-Fill Limit: Maximum quantity per dispensing transaction.
    Example: Sildenafil (Viagra/Revatio) — 6 tablets per 30 days for erectile dysfunction indication.
  Per-Time-Period Limit: Maximum total quantity within a rolling time window.
    Example: Sumatriptan (Imitrex) — 9 tablets per 30 days for migraine.
  Dose-Based Limit: Maximum daily dose tied to clinical guidelines.
    Example: Gabapentin — 3,600 mg/day maximum per FDA label.
  Duration Limit: Drug covered only for a specific treatment duration.
    Example: Antibiotics for acute sinusitis — 10-day supply maximum without PA.

4.2 Common Quantity Limit Examples

Opioids:
  - Short-acting opioids for acute pain (non-cancer): 7-day supply limit per CMS/CDC guidelines (2022).
  - Extended-release opioids: Require PA and quantity documentation from prescriber.

Sleep Aids:
  - Zolpidem (Ambien): 10 tablets per 30 days (standard QL).
  - Eszopiclone (Lunesta): 15 tablets per 30 days.
  - Lemborexant (Dayvigo): 30 tablets per 30 days.

Erectile Dysfunction:
  - PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil): 6–8 tablets per 30 days (varies by plan).
  - Note: Generic tadalafil and sildenafil QLs apply separately from brand versions.

Self-Injected Biologics:
  - Adalimumab (standard dosing): 2 syringes per 28 days (40 mg dosing).
  - Dupilumab (Dupixent): 2 syringes per 28 days (300 mg maintenance).

Oncology:
  - Oral chemotherapy: QLs generally not applied within the cycle-appropriate quantity. Plans must not restrict oral chemo in ways that delay cancer treatment.

4.3 Quantity Limit Exception Process
  Same documentation requirements as tier exceptions (see Part II). Approved QL exceptions typically extend the approved quantity for 12 months with annual renewal.

PART V: PREVENTIVE DRUG COVERAGE (ACA MANDATE)

Under the Affordable Care Act (ACA), non-grandfathered commercial health plans must cover certain preventive medications at $0 cost sharing.

Currently mandated $0 preventive coverage (select examples):
  - Statins (primary prevention): For adults aged 40–75 with 10% or greater 10-year CVD risk.
  - Aspirin (low-dose): For adults aged 50–59 at 10% or greater 10-year CVD risk.
  - PrEP (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis for HIV): emtricitabine/tenofovir (Truvada/Descovy) — all ages.
  - Contraceptives: All FDA-approved contraceptive methods, at $0 for women.
  - Tobacco cessation: Medications approved for smoking cessation (varenicline, bupropion, NRT).
  - Colorectal cancer screening agents: Bowel preparation medications.

Note: The ACA preventive coverage mandate applies to in-network services. CMS has ongoing litigation clarifying which USPSTF recommendations require $0 cost sharing.

PART VI: INSULIN AFFORDABILITY (PART D — INFLATION REDUCTION ACT)

Effective January 1, 2023 (for Part D), and January 1, 2024 (for commercial plans with HHS guidance):

  - Member cost sharing for covered insulin products is capped at $35 per month per covered insulin product.
  - This applies regardless of the deductible phase or formulary tier.
  - The $35 cap applies to a 30-day supply; for 90-day supplies, the cap is $105.
  - Plans may not apply deductibles to insulin products subject to the cap.

Insulin products subject to the cap must be on the plan's formulary. Non-formulary insulins are not subject to the $35 cap.
